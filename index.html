About my work

Questions that connect daily life to wider structures.

My dissertation examined how work, family, and personal time interact as a system, and how those patterns reproduce gender and class inequality. I bring quantitative and computational methods to nationally representative surveys, occupational data, and other complex sources.

My collaborative work spans adolescent health and preventive care, migration and environmental hazards, and student interactions with AI. Across these projects, I’m interested in the link between individual decisions and the social conditions that make them possible.
